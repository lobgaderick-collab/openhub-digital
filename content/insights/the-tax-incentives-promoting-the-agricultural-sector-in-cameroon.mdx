The agricultural sector plays a vital role in the economic development of Cameroon. To promote and support companies involved in agriculture, stock breeding, and fisheries, the General Tax Code of Cameroon includes specific tax incentives for the agricultural sector. 

Section 122 of the General Tax Code outlines these incentives, which aim to encourage investment in the agricultural sector. 

In this article (Part I), we will explore in detail some of the tax incentives provided to companies in the agricultural sector during the **investment phase**.

### Tax Incentives for the Agricultural Sector

Companies involved in agriculture, stock breeding, and fisheries can benefit from various tax incentives as outlined in Section 122 of the General Tax Code for Cameroon. 

These incentives are designed to reduce the financial burden on agricultural companies and encourage investment in the sector. Let's explore each of these incentives in detail:

### 1\. Waiver from taxes and contributions on wages paid to seasonal agricultural workers

One of the tax incentives provided to companies in the agricultural sector is the waiver from taxes and contributions on wages paid to seasonal agricultural workers. 

This incentive aims to support companies by reducing the financial burden associated with employing seasonal workers. 

By waiving taxes and contributions on wages, companies can allocate more resources towards hiring and retaining skilled agricultural workers. This not only benefits the companies but also promotes employment opportunities in rural areas.

### 2\. Exemption from VAT on the purchase of pesticides, fertilizers, and inputs

To further support agricultural activities, companies involved in agriculture, stock breeding, and fisheries are exempted from paying Value Added Tax (VAT) on the purchase of certain essential items. 

This includes pesticides, fertilizers, and inputs used by farmers, as well as equipment and materials listed in the Annex attached to this part in the [General Tax Code](https://openhubdigital.com/store/product/2023-general-tax-code-for-cameroon-in-english/). 

This exemption helps reduce the overall cost of production for farmers and promotes sustainable farming practices. It also encourages the use of quality inputs, leading to improved agricultural productivity.

### 3\. Exemption from registration fees on transfers of land

Agricultural companies often require land for their operations, and transferring land can involve significant administrative and financial burdens. 

Recognizing this, the General Tax Code provides an exemption from registration fees on transfers of land used for agriculture, stock breeding, and pisciculture. 

This incentive aims to alleviate the financial burden associated with land transactions and facilitates the expansion of agricultural activities. It encourages landowners to lease or sell their land for agricultural purposes, promoting the growth of the sector.

### 4\. Exemption from registration fees for loan agreements

Access to finance is crucial for the growth and development of agricultural companies. To make financing more accessible, companies involved in agriculture, stock breeding, and fishing are exempted from paying registration fees for loan agreements. 

This exemption reduces the financial burden associated with borrowing capital for agricultural purposes. It encourages companies to seek financing for investments in infrastructure, equipment, and technology, ultimately promoting the modernization and expansion of the agricultural sector.

### 5\. Exemption from land tax for property used in agricultural activities

Agricultural companies often own land specifically used for agriculture, stock breeding, and fishing. 

The General Tax Code provides an exemption from land tax for property belonging to agriculture, stock breeding, and fishing companies, which is used for these activities, excluding office buildings. 

This exemption helps reduce the operating costs for agricultural companies and incentivizes investment in the sector. It allows companies to allocate their resources towards improving agricultural practices and increasing productivity rather than paying a substantial amount in land taxes.

The tax incentives outlined in Section 122 of the General Tax Code of Cameroon aim to promote investment, reduce production costs, and support the growth of companies involved in agriculture, stock breeding, and fisheries. 

These incentives provide companies with financial relief, encourage employment opportunities, and facilitate access to finance. 

By creating an enabling environment for the agricultural sector, the government of Cameroon aims to foster sustainable agricultural practices, enhance food security, and contribute to the overall economic development of the country.
