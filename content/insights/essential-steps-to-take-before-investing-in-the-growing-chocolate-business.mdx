### 2\. Understanding the Export Duty Policies

The export duty policies of the government of Cameroon should also be understood before investing in the chocolate market. It is important to understand the implications of the export duty and how it will affect profits.

Whether you are a business, a manufacturer, or a consumer, understanding the export duty policies on raw materials is important.

Export duties are taxes imposed on the export of goods and services from one country to another. They are the same in principle as import duties, which are taxes imposed on the import of goods and services from one country to another.

### Why Do Governments Impose Export Duties?

#### i. Protect domestic industries

Export duties are important for two reasons: to protect domestic industry from unfair foreign competition and to generate revenue for the government. The main purpose of export duties is to make it more expensive for foreign competitors to export goods to countries where domestic production of those goods would otherwise be profitable. As such, export duties are commonly used to protect domestic industries from foreign competition.

#### ii. Regulate international trade

Export duties can also be used to regulate international trade. This means that some countries may impose export duties on goods that they have an interest in keeping domestic. For example, if a country has limited resources and needs to protect its domestic industry, it might impose export duties to limit the amount of foreign goods entering the domestic market. This can help the domestic industry to remain competitive and protect its domestic market.

#### iii. Raise revenue for the government

Export duties can also be used to raise revenue for the government. Export duties can be used to fund government programs and services, such as infrastructure, health care, and education. In this way, export duties can help to keep the public sector afloat while providing services and programs to citizens.

#### iv. Discourage certain types of trade

Export duties can also be used to discourage certain types of trade. For example, some countries may impose export duties on goods that they consider to be environmentally damaging, such as certain types of hazardous materials. This can help to protect the environment and discourage the importation of goods that may be damaging to the environment.

Understanding the different types of export duty policies, and the purpose behind them, is essential for businesses, manufacturers, and consumers alike. Knowing how export duties work can help businesses and manufacturers make informed decisions on which markets to enter and which goods to import or export.

Consumers can also benefit from understanding export duty policies, as it can help them to make informed decisions when purchasing goods from abroad.
