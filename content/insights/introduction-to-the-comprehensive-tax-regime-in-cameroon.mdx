Cameroon’s comprehensive tax regime is a simplified taxation system designed to ease the tax burden on small and medium-sized businesses. Instituted under Section C38 of the local tax law, it brings together different tax obligations into one payment, offering both clarity and convenience to eligible taxpayers.

This new regime provides an accessible alternative that fosters compliance and growth.

### What is the Comprehensive Tax?

The comprehensive tax is a single tax that replaces several traditional taxes, including:

- The business license tax,

- Value-Added Tax (VAT), and

- Personal Income Tax (PIT) for Industrial and Commercial Profits (BIC) and Agricultural Profits (BA).

This system is targeted at small businesses with an annual turnover below 50 million FCFA, allowing them to focus on their core operations rather than going through complicated tax calculations.

By combining these taxes, the comprehensive tax will reduce administrative burdens for both taxpayers and the tax administration.

### Who Benefits from the Comprehensive Tax Regime?

The regime is tailored for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) engaged in commercial, industrial, handicraft, or agro-pastoral activities. Eligibility criteria include:

- An annual turnover of less than 50 million FCFA,

- Businesses not subject to assessment based on actual earnings.

This means that smaller businesses, particularly those in their early growth stages, can benefit from simplified tax processes and predictable payment schedules.

### Advantages of the Comprehensive Tax Regime

1. **Simplification**: Businesses no longer have to file separate returns for business license tax, VAT, and personal income tax. Instead, one declaration suffices.

3. **Cost Efficiency**: The tax rates are scaled to the business’s turnover, ensuring affordability.

5. **Administrative Relief**: The streamlined system reduces paperwork and minimizes the risk of errors.

7. **Encourages Compliance**: By making taxes simpler to understand and pay, more businesses are likely to meet their obligations.

### Payment and Compliance

Payments under this regime are made quarterly, with deadlines set at 15 days after the end of each quarter. Businesses must declare their turnover and pay the appropriate tax electronically through the tax administration’s prescribed system. Failing to meet these deadlines could result in penalties, including fines or even business closure.
